ACTIS stripe run · measure_stripes.py

How tiles are screened

The stripe run cuts every merged page into 128-pixel tiles and measures only the tiles whose hole pattern matches the reference. This page follows one tile through that test and shows what the test cannot see.

Why tiles at a pattern edge can come out bright

  1. A

    Part of a tile may lie past the edge. Up to 23 of a tile's 128 columns can lie outside the pattern and the tile still passes, fewer when that area is much brighter or darker than the pattern. Its raw mean includes those columns.

  2. B

    The test ignores brightness. Each quadrant's mean and contrast are removed before comparing. A tile lit by a bright area nearby scores as well as any other, and its raw mean is higher.

  3. C

    A thin border is never compared. After aligning, a strip of up to about half a hole pitch on one side and one end is left out of the score, but counted in the raw mean.

Each tile is judged alone. Nothing in the run drops a tile for being next to a pattern edge or next to a rejected tile.

One tile, two paths

The five steps

  1. Make the template

    prepare_reference · src/actis/tiff_tiles.py:76

    The reference image is searched for hole lattices. One 128 × 128 tile is cut from the middle of each lattice that is large enough. Every tile of every page is compared with that clean tile.

    More detail

    A lattice must be at least one tile wide and tall, with at least five pitches across the tile. Several lattices give several templates. Each page tile is scored against all of them and keeps the best.

    The template's mean is removed, a Hann window is applied and its spectrum is computed once. The shift search radius is the longest lattice vector rounded up, at most 32 px. For a 6.6 px pitch that is 7 px.

  2. Merge the boards and cut tiles

    merge_boards, cut_tiles · src/actis/stripes.py:114, 152

    The four board pages are joined left to right with the 32 shared columns counted once: 4 × 1,376 − 3 × 32 = 5,408 columns. Tiles are cut from the top-left corner, 42 across and 16 down, 672 per page.

    The grid does not follow the pattern, so a pattern edge can fall anywhere inside a tile. The last 32 columns belong to no tile.

  3. Align the tile with the template

    _match · src/actis/tiff_tiles.py:219

    Tile and template are cross-correlated by FFT. The peak is searched only within the search radius, about one pitch. Among equally high peaks the one nearest zero shift wins. A parabola through the peak gives the sub-pixel shift.

  4. Score the tile

    _quadrant_scores, score_tiles · src/actis/tiff_tiles.py:193, 254

    The template is moved by that shift and laid over the tile. The tile is split into four 64 × 64 quadrants, and in each the Pearson correlation of tile and template is computed over the pixels the moved template covers. The score is the lowest of the four. A score of 0.80 or more accepts the tile (--min-corr).

    Why the lowest of four, and why Pearson

    One correlation over the whole tile would forgive an empty quadrant, because the other three would hold the average up. Taking the lowest makes every quadrant show the pattern. A quadrant with no pattern scores about 0.

    Pearson correlation subtracts each image's mean and divides by its spread. That lets one template work on pages of different exposure and focus. It is also why the score cannot see brightness (B).

  5. Measure

    _raw_means · src/actis/tiff_benchmark.py:230

    An accepted tile's raw value is the mean of all its 16,384 pixels as recorded. A rejected tile gets NaN in the results and −1 in reference. The score and the shift are not saved.

What the score cannot see

A · Part of the tile past the pattern edge

Move a pattern edge into the tile and choose how bright the area past it is. The numbers come from an idealised model of one quadrant pair: the edge runs straight down through the right half, alignment is perfect, there is no noise or blur, and holes cover 25% of each lattice cell (the synthetic geometry; the real hole size is not on record).

The model's formulas

A quadrant whose fraction f shows the pattern, with the rest flat at level c, correlates with the template as r = √f / √(1 + (1 − f) d²), where d = (c − μ) / σ is how far the flat area is from the pattern's mean, in units of the pattern's pixel spread. With holes covering a fraction a of the cell, d = (t − a) / √(a (1 − a)) for brightness t on the 0–100% scale above.

At the pattern's average brightness d = 0 and r = √f, so 0.80 passes down to f = 0.64: 23 of 64 columns past the edge. The raw mean of the tile moves by (k / 128)(t − a) / a of the pattern's signal above background, for k columns past the edge.

On a synthetic page with blur and noise, tiles with 12 px past the edge into a dark area scored 0.82 to 0.90. The model gives 0.87.

A brighter area past the edge lets fewer columns through, but each column lifts the raw mean more. A bright open area 7 px into the tile still passes, and adds about 16% to the pattern's signal above background.

B · Light added to the whole tile

Light that varies across a tile lowers the score only a little. On the synthetic page, tiles under a glow that rose toward a bright pad still scored 0.99 to 1.00, and their raw mean was 12% above that of a tile further in. Holes that print larger near an array edge are invisible to the score for the same reason.

C · The border that is never compared

The moved template does not cover the whole tile. A strip as wide as the shift, up to about half a hole pitch, is left out of every quadrant's correlation on one side and at one end. Those pixels still count in the raw mean. A pattern edge inside that strip goes unnoticed by the score.

Check it on your pages

scripts/show_tile_selection.py runs the same merge, tile grid and screen on one page and draws the verdict of every tile.

python scripts/show_tile_selection.py D:\ACTIS\scan\RAW\S1\1_1_?.tiff --reference holes.tiff --page 120

Give the four board files of a segment to see the merged page the stripe run screened, with the same tile grid. The ? pattern also works in PowerShell. Pages count from 0, like page_index in the results. On a machine without a display, add --save page120.png.

Hover over a tile to see its score, raw mean and shift in the window's status bar.

Telling A from B

The bright edge tiles score between 0.80 and about 0.95, and the pattern visibly stops inside them. A: tiles partly past the edge.
The bright edge tiles score near 1.00, the pattern fills them, and the hover shows a higher raw mean. B: real extra light, or larger holes at the edge.

Running the viewer with --min-corr 0.95 shows which tiles a stricter threshold would drop, without measuring the stripe again.
